Collecting emails through the quiz

Five phases to a marketing list that is lawful, that fills itself from the moment people are happiest, and that you can export and send from on day one.

Not legal advice. The code is built to satisfy UK GDPR and PECR as I read them; the wording customers see is the part worth twenty minutes of a solicitor's time — and the cheapest part to change, since it is versioned data rather than markup.

Over half of this already exists

The plumbing for marketing email was built for the promotional strip at the foot of receipts. It assumes the person already bought something and has an account. A quiz-taker has neither — that gap is the whole job.

Four rules, and what each one changes

1

Marketing to a non-customer needs a real opt-in

Unticked, separate from the terms, and — the part usually missed — the email has to work without it. If ticking is the price of getting your stack, the consent isn't freely given and the whole list is built on sand.

UK GDPR Art. 4(11) · PECR reg. 22 → Phase 1's headline test: submit unticked, still get the stack.

2

A customer is a different case

Someone who bought can be marketed similar products under the soft opt-in, provided every message can be refused. So each address records which basis applies — they are not interchangeable, and a list that forgot can only be treated as the stricter one.

PECR reg. 22(3) → a basis column, not a boolean.

3

Tell them at the field, not in a policy nobody opens

Who we are, what we do with it, how long we keep it, how to stop — linked and legible at the point of collection.

Art. 13 → the privacy notice is Phase 0, not a later tidy-up.

4

Stopping must be as easy as starting

One click, no login, no form. That already ships — what it doesn't yet do is cover someone who never had an account, or separate "stop the marketing" from "stop everything", which matters because receipts can't stop.

Art. 7(3) → Phase 4's preference centre.

Where the ask goes

On the reveal, underneath the stack they have just been given. It is the one moment where an email address is a favour to them — save this, don't lose it — rather than a toll on the way to something they want.

The rule the card is built against: it never gates anything. No modal, no "enter your email to see your results", no disabled button. The quiz completes, prices and checks out identically for someone who ignores it.

The phases

Phase 0

The legal spine

A privacy notice, somewhere to put a non-customer, and one function that answers "may we market to this address?" for leads and members alike. No capture UI — this is what makes the next phase lawful.

Proves it worksA consent record can re-render the exact sentence the person saw and match its hash — the same property the checkout consents already have.

1.5 daysRisk: low — additiveDepends on: nothing
Phase 1

The capture

The card above, live on the reveal: a rate-limited endpoint, the stack email itself as a new template, consent evidence written with the statement version and hash, and funnel events so we can see what it does.

Proves it worksSubmit with the box unticked → the stack email arrives and no marketing consent is written. Then the opt-out link in that email suppresses the address, end to end, in the browser suite.

2 daysRisk: medium — new UI on the best screen we haveGuardrail: reveal → checkout not down
Phase 2

A second moment, one identity

The build screen's 3.4 seconds of dead time gets the same field, behind a flag so it is measured rather than assumed — it sits before the payoff, which is the shape that can cost conversion. Buyers get recorded under the soft opt-in at the account gate, and a lead who later signs up becomes one record with one preference.

Proves it worksOpting out anywhere opts out everywhere, and the flag off is today's behaviour byte for byte.

1.5 daysRisk: medium — the conversion-sensitive oneDepends on: Phase 1
Phase 3

Seeing them, and getting them out

Founders Hub → Audience: counts across the top, a filterable list below, and a CSV export. Every exported row carries its own unsubscribe link — so a campaign sent from Gmail or Mailchimp can drop a working opt-out into the footer as a merge field, and the opt-out lands back in our database where the next export already excludes it.

Proves it worksA suppressed address can never appear in a default export, and every export is logged: who, when, how many, which filter.

1.5 daysRisk: lowCan jump ahead of Phase 2
Phase 4

Sending, and the opt-out that has to work

A preference centre behind the existing one-click link — stop marketing, or stop everything that isn't the service — that works for someone with no account. Plus the one-click unsubscribe headers Gmail and Yahoo have required from bulk senders since 2024. Optionally, sending from the hub itself, re-checking suppression at send time rather than compose time.

Proves it worksAn address that opts out between composing and sending is not sent to — and nobody loses a receipt by unsubscribing from marketing.

2–3 daysHalves if we send from GmailRisk: medium — real inboxes
Phase 5

Rights, retention, hygiene

The obligations that arrive with a list and don't announce themselves: an email-preferences row in My Hub, a retention purge on the nightly job, and one-button answers to "send me everything you hold" and "delete me".

Proves it worksErasure removes the lead but keeps the opt-out record — deleting that would let the address be re-added and re-emailed, which is the opposite of what they asked for.

1.5 daysRisk: lowDepends on: Phase 3

Three things I need from you

None of them block Phase 0 starting.

How long do we keep an address that never buys?

24 months is the usual answer and what the notice will say unless you'd rather it were shorter.

Double opt-in, or single?

Confirming by email costs 20–30% of sign-ups and buys cleaner deliverability. My recommendation: single now, with the switch built so it's a config change if the list ever gets used hard.

The privacy notice's specifics.

The registered entity and address, whether we're registered with the ICO (a controller doing direct marketing generally must be — around £52 a year), and a confirmed processor list: Stripe, PowerBody, Google Workspace or Resend, Vercel, OpenAI.

Order and effort

Phases 0 and 1 together are the smallest thing worth shipping: a lawful list that fills itself from the reveal. Phase 3 is what makes it useful to you, and can jump the queue if you'd rather have the export than the second capture point.
